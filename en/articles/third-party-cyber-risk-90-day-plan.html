Third-Party Cyber Risk: A 90-Day Plan for Thai Organizations

Turn supplier reviews into decisions that connect risk, critical services, and accountable owners.

Published 21 September 2026 · 6 min read

LinkedInFacebookX

Organizations rely increasingly on cloud, SaaS, contractors, and digital partners. Risk no longer ends at the enterprise boundary. Effective supplier assessment starts with critical services and business impact, not one generic questionnaire for every provider.

Key takeaways

  • Tier suppliers by impact on critical services first, then choose assessment depth. One questionnaire for everyone wastes effort.
  • Ninety days is enough for a decision-ready baseline: a register, tiering, control gaps, and a remediation plan with owners.
  • Use ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.19–5.23 as the baseline controls, and PDPA sections 37(4) and 40 for personal data.
  • The executive metric is residual risk to critical services, not the number of questionnaires returned.

Start with the suppliers that matter most

Segment suppliers by their access to personal or sensitive data, system connectivity, dependency for customer-facing services, and substitutability. Ask what an outage or incident would mean for customers, revenue, and regulatory obligations. This determines the assessment and monitoring level.

A 90-day plan for a decision-ready baseline

  1. Days 1–30: Build a supplier register linked to services, data, systems, and relationship owners; identify critical suppliers.
  2. Days 31–60: Assess risk by criticality, review control evidence, and identify gaps in security, privacy, resilience, and incident response.
  3. Days 61–90: Create remediation plans with owners, dates, and risk-acceptance criteria; establish executive review for critical suppliers.

Controls to ask for and test

  • Access management, encryption, and data segregation appropriate to the service.
  • Incident-notification timing and joint response arrangements.
  • Continuity, recovery, and testing evidence for critical services.
  • Subcontractor controls and a right to know about material changes.

What executives should see

A useful report is not a collection of questionnaire scores. It shows critical suppliers, residual risk, service impact, decisions required, and remediation progress. This helps cybersecurity, procurement, legal, and service owners make decisions together.

A Thai scenario: when the supplier is breached and you are the last to know

A retailer's payroll processor was hit by ransomware on a Friday night. The company learned about it on Monday morning from the news, not from the supplier. Over ten thousand employee records sat in that system, and the contract set no notification deadline.

The consequence was not only a potential fine. The organization could not notify the Personal Data Protection Committee office within the 72 hours required by section 37(4), because it did not know the scope of the data affected and had no contractual right to the supplier's investigation report.

Frequently asked questions

What is third-party cyber risk?

It is risk arising from external providers, partners, or software that access important organizational data, systems, or processes.

How often should suppliers be assessed?

Set frequency by criticality and service change. Critical suppliers need ongoing monitoring and review when material changes occur.

How many suppliers should we start with?

Start with the 20–40 Tier 1 suppliers that directly affect critical services. In most organizations fewer than 10 percent of suppliers carry most of the risk, and assessing everyone at once rarely finishes within 90 days.

Is a supplier's ISO/IEC 27001 certificate enough?

Not on its own. Check that the certificate scope covers the service actually delivered to you, and read the Statement of Applicability for excluded controls. A certificate covering head office but not the data centre that runs your service is a common finding.

What notification deadline should the contract set?

Require notification within 24 hours of the supplier becoming aware, so the organization still has time to scope the incident and notify the regulator within the 72 hours set by PDPA section 37(4). Setting the supplier deadline at 72 hours leaves the organization no time at all.
